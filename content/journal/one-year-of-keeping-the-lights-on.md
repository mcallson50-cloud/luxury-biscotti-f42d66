We opened a year ago this week with four chairs too many and no idea where people would want to sit. The plan had the long table against the back wall, out of the way, because we assumed strangers would not want to share. That table is now the first thing to fill, every day, and the two-seaters by the window sit empty until eleven.

So we moved it forward. It took three of us and a removed door frame.

## What changed

The counter is eighty centimetres lower than it was. That sounds like a detail and it is the single biggest thing we have done — you can see the hands, the scale, the puck being knocked out. People ask what is going on and then we get to answer. At the old height, everyone just waited.

We also stopped writing the filter offering on a board by the door. Nobody read it. Now whoever is on bar says it out loud, once, to each person: *there is a washed Sumatra and a natural Ethiopian, the Ethiopian tastes like apricot.* Filter sales roughly doubled.

> A café is not a room with coffee in it. It is a set of small decisions about where people can put their elbows.

## What did not

The mint on the windowsill has been replaced four times. Everyone touches the leaves on the way past — we counted, on a slow Tuesday, out of curiosity — and no plant survives that much affection. We have accepted this as a recurring cost.

We still do not have a second grinder, which means the espresso and the decaf share one, which means there is a fifteen-second purge every time someone orders decaf. Nine people a day order decaf. That is two and a half minutes of coffee thrown away daily. The grinder is ordered.

## The regulars

Eleven people come in before eight. They order the same thing every day and we have stopped asking. Two of them have never told us their names and it feels too late now. One brings his own cup, a chipped green thing, and gets a fifty-cent discount that he insists on rounding back up in the tip jar.

This is the part nobody warned us about: you build a room for the idea of people, and then actual people arrive and quietly rearrange it around themselves. The clothing came out of that. Half the reason Capsule 01 exists is that the aprons and shirts we made for ourselves kept getting asked about across the counter.

Year two: the second grinder, a proper cupping table on Wednesdays, and the waxed apron that has been a prototype for far too long.
