The first thing we ever made was an apron, and we made it badly. Wrong hardware, strap that slid off a shoulder every time someone reached for a portafilter. But it was olive canvas and it looked like it belonged in the room, and within a fortnight three separate people had asked if they could buy one.

That is the entire origin of Capsule 01. Not a brand strategy. A badly made apron.

## The brief we actually wrote

We wrote it on the back of an invoice: *clothes for eight hours behind a counter, or a whole afternoon at a table.* That is a narrower brief than most collections start from, and it made every decision easier.

- Nothing that shows a milk splash immediately, which killed pure white early on.
- Nothing that needs ironing, because none of us iron.
- Nothing with a logo big enough to read from across the room.
- Fabric heavy enough to survive being washed twice a week for a year.

The last one is why the tee is 240gsm. We went through four samples. The first three were lovely and looked exhausted after two shifts.

## Colour came from the room

We did not build a mood board. We photographed the café at four in the afternoon — the tiles behind the counter, the unglazed terracotta, the paper cups, that specific sage of the mint on the windowsill — and pulled the palette straight off the photographs. Bone, sage, clay, terracotta, tobacco.

> If a piece does not look right sitting on one of our own chairs, it is not in the capsule.

Everything is either undyed or pigment-dyed, which means the sage overshirt will fade unevenly at the elbows and cuffs. We had a long argument about whether to warn people about this. We decided to say it plainly instead: it will fade, that is the material being honest about how much you wore it.

## Why 120

A run of 120 per piece is small enough that we could pay for it out of the café's own margin, without an investor and without pre-orders. It is also small enough that we can stop. If Capsule 02 does not have a reason to exist, there will not be a Capsule 02 — the next thing we make will be whatever people keep asking us for across the counter.

The apron, by the way, is being re-cut in waxed 12oz canvas with a cross-back strap that stays put. It is the one piece from the original set that is still not for sale, which is a fitting kind of joke.
