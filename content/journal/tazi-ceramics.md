Hafida Tazi works out of a studio two U-Bahn stops from here, and for the last eight months every cortado glass, filter cup and saucer in the café has come off her wheel. A hundred and forty pieces so far. We have broken nineteen.

## The mistake we made first

We asked her for a hundred identical cups. She said yes, and then, a week later, sent an email that said roughly: *I can do that, but you will not like them.*

She was right. The first batch came back matched to within a few grams and they felt like tableware from a catalogue — technically correct, completely inert. Nobody picked one up and turned it over. So we scrapped the brief and asked for the opposite: same glaze, same rough capacity, weights and profiles free to wander.

> The point of a handmade cup is not that it looks handmade. It is that you notice you are holding a specific object.

Now the cups vary by up to fifteen grams and the rims run from thin to noticeably thick. Regulars have favourites and will wait for one to come out of the dishwasher. Two people have offered to buy specific cups. We have said no, so far.

## The glaze

Unglazed terracotta on the outside, cream slip inside, no gloss anywhere. The clay is a local red that fires to almost exactly the colour of the tiles behind our counter, which is not a coincidence — Hafida matched it off a broken tile we handed her in a paper bag.

It is also the colour that ended up on the Zellige Bomber, so the collaboration has quietly leaked into the clothing.

## Coming in October

Hafida is throwing a run of 60 cups for sale, in two sizes: a 90ml for espresso and nass-nass, and a 200ml for filter. Sold in the café only, not online, because packing thin-rimmed ceramics for post is a problem neither of us wants to solve this year.

She is also running a wheel session in the studio on a Saturday morning in October — eight seats, ninety minutes, you take home whatever you make and we fire it for you. Dates go on the journal and the newsletter first.
