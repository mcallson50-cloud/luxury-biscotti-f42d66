In Morocco, if you do not want a black coffee and you do not want a milky one, you ask for *nass-nass* — half-half. It arrives in a small glass, roughly equal parts coffee and hot milk, and nobody asks you to specify a size or a milk or a syrup. It is the least fussy way to order coffee I know.

We put it on the board in month three, mostly as a private joke, and it is now the second most ordered drink in the café.

## Why it works

A cortado is 60ml of milk on a double shot. A latte is 200ml or more. There is an enormous gap between those two numbers and most people live inside it — they want the coffee to still taste like coffee, but they want enough milk that it is a drink and not a shot.

Nass-nass is about 90ml of milk, steamed to roughly 60°C rather than the usual 65, in a 150ml glass. Lower temperature because there is no foam to hold and because you should be able to drink it immediately. That is the whole spec.

> Most menu innovation is a new syrup. This one was just a different ratio with a name attached.

## The espresso underneath

Ours is pulled shorter and sweeter than is currently fashionable: 18g in, 34g out, 27 seconds. That is a low ratio by specialty standards, and if you drink it straight it is thick and slightly jammy. We are not optimising for straight espresso, though — four out of five shots we pull go into milk, so we dial for what happens after the milk arrives.

Two origins at a time, nothing resting longer than nine days, roasted twenty minutes east of here. The washed Sumatra is doing the espresso work at the moment; plum and cocoa, and it holds up against milk without disappearing.

## If you want to try it properly

Order it at the counter and drink it standing up, which is how it is drunk at home. It will take you four minutes. Then order a filter and sit down for an hour — the two things are not competing.
